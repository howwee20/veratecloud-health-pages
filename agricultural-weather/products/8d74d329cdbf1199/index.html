.DISCUSSION... 
Issued at 305 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
The short wave trough and pre-frontal forcing which aided in showers  
along and south of the US Route 24 corridor earlier today will  
continue to shift across the eastern Great Lakes region this  
evening. Isolated showers and thunderstorms have also developed  
during the mid afternoon across southwest Lower Michigan/far  
northern Indiana where some increase in low level frontogenesis  
forcing allowed development along weak confluence zone and also  
just downstream of what appears to be southwest extent of weak  
DPVA. 
 
The focus for tonight into early Wednesday will be another low  
amplitude short wave trough ejecting out of the Dakotas and a  
resultant slow southward sinking baroclinic zone into  
Wednesday. Preceding this wave and frontal zone will be another  
surge of higher low level theta-e air. Isentropic forcing does  
not appear to be as pronounced as last night, but axis of fairly 
strong low level fgen forcing and increasing elevated  
instability should compensate for this with scattered showers  
and isolated/scattered thunderstorms expected to redevelop late  
evening into the overnight hours. Given elevated nature of the  
instability, severe weather is not expected. Some brief heavy  
rainfall is possible given increasing depth of moisture  
profiles, long/skinny CAPE profiles, and increasing warm cloud  
depths. Given quite dry antecedent conditions, significant  
hydro impacts are not expected. 
 
By mid afternoon Wednesday, scattered shower/storm activity should  
progressively sink southward as low level fgen axis becomes more  
muted and shifted south. This will occur as some recovery in mid  
level heights occurs upstream in response to stronger upper  
trough/cut-off low feature lifting northeast out of the Four Corners  
region.  
 
Another round of active weather is expected Thursday and Friday as  
the cut-off upper low begins to phase with a more dynamic northern  
stream upper level trough digging southeast out of south central  
Canada. This phasing system will likely result in cyclogenesis  
overhead late Thursday with some favorable upper jet dynamics  
also coming into play. This strongly forced setup should support 
another round of showers and thunderstorms Thursday afternoon  
and Thursday night before a strong accompanying cold front  
sweeps across the area. With the tendency to signals of better  
phasing, sfc reflection is likely to take a track closer to  
guidance that has been advertising a northerly track across  
IN/NW OH/SE Lower MI. This could provide a brief temporal window 
of weak near surface based CAPE across at least southeast  
portions of the forecast area late Thursday afternoon and more  
likely Thursday evening. Uncertainty remains with just how far  
north warm frontal feature can progress Thursday night but given 
robust kinematic profiles may need to monitor for isolated  
strong/severe threat Thursday evening, especially south of US  
24. Given progression of features and dry antecedent conditions, 
widespread hydro concerns are not expected. PWATs will be  
highly anomalous on Thursday however, so some localized heavier  
rainfall amounts are possible. 
 
Friday still appears dismal with blustery conditions developing on  
cold side of this deepening system. Peak afternoon gusts into the 35  
to 40 mph range still appear possible given deep post-frontal  
mixing. Some consideration may be given to a wind advisory  
downwind of Lake Michigan, but uncertainty remains in this  
regard. Some lake effect rain showers will also be likely  
although duration of deeper lake induced instability profiles  
appears somewhat limited to Friday morning/early PM. Friday and  
Saturday will feature temps well below normal with nighttime  
lows in the 30s. Along with the better phased system comes the  
potential of a slightly quicker eastward progression of sfc  
anticyclone late Friday night with an attendant frost potential 
Friday night and Saturday night. Flow deamplification quickly  
ensues to start next work week which should result in  
temperatures moderating back to slightly above normal by Monday- 
Tuesday.

.AVIATION /00Z TAFS THROUGH 00Z THURSDAY/... 
Issued at 709 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
Existing TAFs remain on track this evening. A cold front is 
slowly working its way through northern Indiana. Encountering a 
subtropical high over the Southeast, a very weak pressure 
gradient will result in light wind into the overnight hours.  
 
Isolated showers and thunderstorms have festered upstream in the 
vicinity of the front and this activity is expected to  
rejuvenate through early Wednesday morning. Showers become  
divorced from the best upper-level support by sunrise Wednesday 
resulting in a weakening trend toward KFWA. High PWATs can  
likely result in periods of MVFR visibility from any showers  
that move through.
