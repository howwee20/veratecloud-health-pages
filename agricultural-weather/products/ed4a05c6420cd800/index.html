.DISCUSSION... 
Issued at 316 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
Afternoon GOES satellite imagery reveals mostly zonal flow aloft  
with some weak embedded shortwave action skirting through the  
region. Lingering moisture and northerly flow on the backside of a  
departing surface trough is keeping cloudy conditions overhead with  
areas of fog and drizzle present across the upslope locations of the  
west and central. High temperatures have only managed to peak into  
the 40s to low 50s for much of the northern tier of the UP while  
areas of the south-central climb into the mid to upper 50s. A few  
light lake enhanced showers are possible tonight across the north  
and east where 850 mb temps cool between 1-3C. Otherwise, dry and  
seasonable weather holds through much of the mid-week under a period  
of mid-level ridging and surface high pressure. Temps tomorrow look  
very similar to today though some clearing and dry surface air will  
allow overnight temps to fall into the 30s and 40s overnight. 
 
The main concern of this forecast period continues to be the  
evolution of a surface low in the Great Lakes driven by two phasing  
troughs Thursday night through Friday. Operational deterministic  
models and their ensemble counterparts are in pretty good agreement  
that a ~1009 mb surface low will be placed in the vicinity of  
southern Lake Michigan by Thursday evening. Ample northward moisture  
transport and isentropic ascent ahead of the lead northern stream  
trough will provide enough support for widespread rain lifting into  
the UP through Thursday afternoon through Friday morning and latest  
NBM suggests at least a 50-80% chance for QPF >0.25". Early Friday  
morning through Fri afternoon the surface low feature is slated to  
work northeast atop the Lower Lakes, placing Upper Michigan on the  
northwestern flank. Strong mid to level cold air advection and rapid  
pressure rises (upwards of 1.5-2mb/hr) will contribute to northerly  
wind speeds reaching 25-35 kts gusting to near 50 kts on Lake  
Superior and the adjacent lakeshores. There is some uncertainty  
regarding the maximum gust potential as ensembles (NBM and ENS) have  
moderate to high probability (50-70% chance) of gusts >50 kts  
whereas model soundings reveal peak mixing largely near 45 kts. Lake  
effect/enhanced rain on the backside of the system will provide an  
additional 0.1-0.25" of precip once system rain clear the area.  
Wouldn't surprise me to see some wet snow mix into areas of the  
interior highlands early Friday morning before temperature warm into  
the 40s Friday afternoon. No accumulations expected. As winds settle  
late Friday and dry high pressure clears skies from west to east,  
temperatures will drop like a rock overnight into Saturday morning,  
reaching the mid to low 20s for much if not all of the interior  
while the lakeshores may reach freezing for the first time this  
fall. Ridging and dry weather dominate through the rest of the  
weekend with the potential for another warm up / fire weather  
pattern Sunday into Monday.

.AVIATION /00Z TAFS THROUGH 00Z WEDNESDAY/... 
Issued at 715 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
Abundant low level moisture in the wake of last night's disturbance  
continues to leave low ceilings and temporary bouts of low visby at  
all sites overnight tonight. A gradual improvement to MVFR and  
eventually VFR is expected at all sites as high pressure builds in  
from the west with chances of IFR or lower conditions after 07Z at  
less than 1-in-3. Winds remain generally northwesterly at around 5- 
10 kt with gusts to around 10-15 kt. 
 
&& 
 
.MARINE... 
Issued at 316 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
Lake Superior: Periods of breezy 15-25 kt NW gusts hold through  
Thursday before becoming southerly ahead of a strong cold front  
Thursday night into Friday morning. This strong cold front  
accompanies a deepening low pressure system that works through the  
Lower Great Lakes on Friday. Strong cold advection and rapid  
pressure rises will create sustained winds near gale force with  
gusts reaching 45-55 kts in the central lake Friday morning.  
Significant wave heights build between 15-20 feet, highest along the  
southern lakeshores. Winds and waves slowly subside lake into early  
Saturday. 
 
Northern Green Bay and northwestern Lake Michigan: Breezy NW winds  
near 15-20 kts remain through Thursday ahead of a strong cold front  
that whips through the northern bay and lake late Thurs-early Fri.  
Northwest winds quickly ramp up between 35-40 kts Friday with waves  
building between 5-8 ft. Winds and waves subside below gale force  
into Saturday.
