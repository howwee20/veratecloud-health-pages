.PREV DISCUSSION... 
Issued at 346 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
DISCUSSION... 
 
Aggressive low and mid level drying outpaced last evening's 00Z  
models, yielding clear skies since the mid-morning hours. GOES water  
vapor imagery continues to show widespread subsaturated air  
encompassing the Great Lakes while the exiting low's cold front  
slowly clears toward the Michigan/Ohio border. Taking into  
consideration the slow frontal progress, uninhibited insolation, and  
850 mb temperatures hovering near 17C over Metro Detroit, recording  
breaking heat remains in reach today, given the previous record of  
just 83F. Further north, post-frontal flow has flipped west- 
northwesterly which will spread southeastward through the evening  
hours. The near-surface layer along the frontal slope cools faster  
than the rest of lower column, leading to a more abrupt end to the  
breezy afternoon conditions. Wind speeds drop off late tonight as  
pressure gradient influence shifts from the low to an upstream ridge  
of high pressure.  
 
The 595 dam 500 mb Gulf Coast ridge remains anchored tonight as a  
plume of 800-600 mb moisture releases anticyclonically from the  
eastern Plains into the Ohio Valley amidst backing westerly flow.  
Meanwhile, the frontal boundary stalls just south of the state line  
as perturbed flow starts to work through aloft, helping trigger a  
period of scattered showers. Models preserve a relatively narrow QPF  
footprint with the highest totals (up to 0.25 inches) along/south of  
I-94, mainly over the southern four counties. Light/trace amounts  
are possible further north, up to M-59. In spite of RRFS forecast  
soundings showing an inversion layer from 2.5-6.5 kft, lapse rates  
above should suffice in generating pockets of 100-250 J/kg of  
elevated CAPE. As a result, a few rumbles of thunder cannot be ruled  
out.  
 
There could still be a few lingering showers exiting the area by  
sunrise Wednesday, followed by mainly dry conditions settling for  
the rest of the daylight hours. Broad surface high pressure  
influence extending from Manitoba to Southeast Michigan ushers in a  
cooler continental airmass. The resulting shift to northerly flow  
helps to nudge the existing vicinity boundary further into the Ohio  
Valley throughout the day. Weaker winds expected as split-flow  
ensues below 700 mb, limiting gusts to less than 15 mph. The next  
round of showers arrives Thursday as a southern stream closed low  
lifts from the desert southwest into the Plains, phasing with a  
digging positively tilted longer wavelength trough. Supporting  
dynamics and a spike in upper level divergence will engage a robust  
theta-e plume lifting up the Mid-Mississippi Valley and eventually  
bringing rain to southern Lower Michigan. High uncertainty exists in  
the onset time for rain, reflected by the gradual northeastward  
expansion of Chance PoPs after 12Z, per NBM. The bulk of the  
rainfall should occur Thursday evening, or early Thursday night as  
the northeast flank passes over Southeast Michigan, well downstream  
of the deepening surface low, still located near the southern shores  
of Lake Michigan. The low then traverses southern Lower Michigan  
late Thursday night before crossing into Lake Huron Friday morning. 
 
Turning colder and much windier on Friday as the system's cold front  
clears through. Aggressive constriction of the pressure gradient is  
possible in its wake, although there is a noticeable difference in  
the magnitudes of the deterministic solutions. The column will cool  
at similar rates, but should see the mixed-layer expand sufficiently  
for 35-45 knot lower tropospheric flow to mix down. Gusts in the 35- 
45 mph range cannot be ruled out Friday afternoon and evening.  
Additionally, a deformation axis trails, which could help spur some  
light scattered rain showers. Chilly overnight with temperatures  
dropping near or slightly below freezing, particularly north of I- 
69. Turning drier through the weekend with slightly warmer readings  
by Sunday, returning to climatological averages. 
 
MARINE... 
  
Conditions remain very stable under southwest flow as air  
temperatures remain warm for this time of year which has tempered  
gust potential. There will be a window this evening where winds back  
to the west as low pressure departs the region. This window of  
backed flow is where elevated mixing depths just will be high enough  
to where gusts around 20 to 25 knots are possible. Small Craft  
Advisories remain in effect from the Saginaw Bay to Port Huron for  
this potential. Any elevated winds will then quickly diminish in the  
later evening hours as the pressure gradient rapidly weakens.  
 
A cold front will then push through the Great Lakes tomorrow,  
stalling across southern Michigan by the afternoon. The favorable  
northwest fetch will produce wind gusts 20 to 30 knots across  
northern Lake Huron. High pressure to then build in through Thursday  
which will bring a period of light winds for the day. Confidence is  
increasing for a strong cold front to pass Friday into Saturday.  
Instability will increase over the lakes in the wake of the front as  
cold air flows over the warmer waters. Coupled with longer duration  
northwest flow, a high-end gale event is looking increasingly likely  
to end the week.
