Prognostic Discussion for 6 to 10 and 8 to 14 day outlooks 
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park, MD 
300 PM EDT Tue October 13 2026 
 
6-10 DAY OUTLOOK FOR OCT 19 - 23 2026 
 
There is good agreement among the dynamical model ensemble solutions depicting  
the mean 500-hPa height anomalies during the 6-10 day period. The model  
guidance indicates good consistency between today and yesterday. A strong  
mid-level positive anomaly and associated ridging is forecast over the eastern  
contiguous U.S. (CONUS). Meanwhile, a weak negative anomaly and mid-level  
troughing is forecast along the West Coast. Further west, positive 500-hPa  
height anomalies are forecast over the north-central Pacific between Hawaii and  
Alaska. This leads to near-normal 500-hPa heights across Hawaii. Guidance is  
indicating mid-level troughing to move into the Bering Sea and Aleutian Islands  
during the period.  
 
Above normal temperatures are favored across much of the CONUS beneath the  
strong mid-level positive height anomalies over the central and eastern U.S.  
Chances for above normal exceed 80% across the north-central CONUS. Below  
normal is the favored outcome over much of the West beneath the 500-hPa  
troughing forecast. In Alaska, a transient mid-level height pattern leads to  
near normal heights supporting near normal temperatures across Mainland Alaska  
with a slight tilt towards above over the Aleutians and Southeast Alaska. In  
Hawaii, above normal temperatures are forecast. 
 
Above normal precipitation is favored across much of the West and into the  
central CONUS corresponding to the increasing strength of the mid-level trough  
over the West. This helps to force strong precipitation chances across much of  
the western and central CONUS with chances exceeding 60% for parts of the   
Great Basin, Four Corners, and into the Southern Plains. Above normal  
precipitation chances have increased relative to yesterday over the Northern  
Plains and Upper Mississippi Valley with chances now exceeding 50% in parts of  
this region. Below normal precipitation is still forecast for much of the  
Northeast, eastern Great Lakes, and Mid-Atlantic ahead of the mid-level  
ridging. In Alaska, above normal precipitation is forecast over the southern  
half of the state. In Hawaii, a slight tilt towards above normal precipitation  
is favored.  
 
The official 6-10 day 500-hPa height blend consists of 10% of Today's 0z GFS  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, 20% of Today's 6z GFS Ensemble Mean centered  
on Day 8, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, and 30%  
of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8  
  
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 6-10 DAY PERIOD: Above Average, 4 out 5, Growing  
confidence in the mid-level height pattern is offset by the precipitation  
anomaly forecast in the West. 
 
8-14 DAY OUTLOOK FOR OCT 21 - 27 2026  
 
As in the 6-10 day period, there is good agreement among the dynamical model  
ensemble solutions depicting the week-2 500-hPa heights. Strong mid-level  
ridging remains in place over the north-central CONUS with positive anomalies  
extending to the Gulf Coast. Mid-level ridging over the Pacific in the 6-10 day  
period has progressed eastwards towards British Columbia and the Pacific  
Northwest. However, there is a stronger signal for continued troughing over the  
southwestern CONUS relative to yesterday. Meanwhile, in Alaska, weak troughing  
is favored to develop. Near normal heights remain forecast in Hawaii.  
 
As in the 6-10 day, above normal temperatures are favored across much of the  
CONUS beneath mid-level positive height anomalies. Chances for above normal  
exceed 70% across the north-central CONUS. Below normal remains the favored  
outcome during week-2 over much of the West beneath 500-hPa troughing. In  
Alaska, weak mid-level ridging over eastern Alaska favors warm southerly flow  
across the eastern Mainland and Southeast Alaska with near Normal over the  
western Mainland. In Hawaii, above normal temperatures are forecast consistent  
with above normal sea surface temperatures in the area. 
 
Above normal precipitation is forecast for much of the western and central  
CONUS consistent with the mid-level troughing in the West and potential  
propagation of shortwave troughing moving into the central CONUS. Near-normal  
precipitation is favored along the Pacific Northwest and Northern California  
coast behind the mid-level trough axis. Near to below normal precipitation is  
also favored in the East with mid-level ridging dominating. In Alaska, above  
normal precipitation is forecast for much of the state consistent with a  
mid-level low over the Aleutians. Above normal precipitation remains favored in  
Hawaii. 
 
The official 8-14 day 500-hPa height blend consists of: 10% of Today's 0z GFS  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, 20% of Today's 6z GFS Ensemble Mean centered  
on Day 11, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, and 30%  
of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11  
 
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 8-14 DAY PERIOD: Average, 3 out of 5, Increased  
confidence in the mid-level height pattern is offset by small precipitation and  
temperature anomalies

Notes: 
 
 
The notation for the categorical forecast indicated on the maps is  
the same as that in the tables: A-above   N-near normal   B-below  
  
The temperature map shows regions with > 33% chance of being warmer 
(orange, "A"), colder (blue, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") normal. 
Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of the more 
likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
The precipitation map shows regions with > 33% chance of being  
wetter (green, "A"), drier (tan, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") 
normal. Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of 
the more likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
In the southwest and other climatologically dry regions - there will  
be a greater than 33.3% chance of no precipitation and occasionally  
even a normal (i.e. Median) value of zero - especially during the dry  
seasons.  In such cases a forecast of near normal is effectively a  
forecast of little or no precipitation.  
  
The climate prediction center uses 1991-2020 base period means as   
reference in the climate outlooks. 
 
The next set of long-lead monthly and seasonal outlooks will be released on  
October 15.
