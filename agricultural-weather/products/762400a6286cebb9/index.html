.DISCUSSION... 
Issued at 247 PM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026 
 
PATTERN SYNOPSIS/FORECAST: 
 
595dm upper level ridge over the southern US, generally centered  
over east TX. General troughing over the western half of the  
continent with a 555dm upper low trying to get cut off over the  
western US...as northern stream energy zips right along through  
southern Canada...with more upper energy across central Canada to  
its north on the nose of a 100kt upper jet. 120kt SW oriented upper  
jet stretches from the Four Corners region up into the Upper MS  
Valley with zonal flow across the Midwest/Great Lakes region. Quite  
a moisture plume present this morning, with pwats 1.5-2in (or more,  
in the case of the S Plains/Lower MS Valley) all the way up into  
IL/IA and southern Ontario near its associated Great Lake. Pwat of  
1.51in on our 12z/13 sounding is a new record for this date here,  
and we are certainly socked in in the low-levels...with fog and low  
clouds across much of the area, continuing to flood in behind a cold  
front swinging through from the west behind a bit of positively  
tilted energy that crossed the region overnight into this morning.  
Attendant surface low sits over GT Bay as of 12z...with cold front  
draping back into KS...and stationary/warm front stretching across  
northern Lower MI, eastward into northern NY. 850mb temp gradient  
from 0C over ND to +20C over SW Lower MI. Westerly(ish) LLJ leading  
to some subtle cold advection across the Upper Midwest into northern  
MI, and some signals of LL moisture trying to mix out upstream...  
but aforementioned stationary boundary and cloud cover holding tight  
here through 15z.  
 
Energy to continue to slide by to our north through the rest of the  
day into tonight...allowing the boundary to slowly drape south  
through the area with time. Zonal boundary should remain stretched  
across the central/eastern US...but will be looking for the ridge  
upstream to build eastward Wednesday ahead of that upper low over  
the Four Corners...leaving us generally under the influence of  
subsidence and high pressure for midweek. The Pacific should spit  
another PV max eastward midweek...and current signals are for this  
to try to absorb that southwest upper low which should also start to  
move eastward a bit. Current expectation is for this to occur just  
upstream over the Upper Midwest...but we will be in the vicinity of  
lingering east-west baroclinic zone, leaving concerns for a potent  
surface low to congeal/spin-up over the Great Lakes Thursday into  
Friday. Think primary concerns with this feature are heavy rain and  
strong winds to wrap up the work week...though it will be cool again  
behind this. High pressure looks to build in for the weekend, with  
signals pointing toward a return to ridging across the eastern US as  
troughing digs into the western US again early next week.  
 
FORECAST DETAILS... 
 
Grungy into tonight...Grungy conditions likely to continue with low- 
level moisture hanging on. Falling temps aloft through the  
afternoon/evening should support some overlake instability,  
particularly across the EUP, where there is a better chance of  
showers hanging on tonight on north/northwest flow. We may struggle  
to fully mix out the low-level moisture through much of the day on  
Wednesday, per guidance soundings, but think cloud bases will rise a  
bit compared to today. Do have concerns for fog to hang on at times,  
particularly Wednesday night as high pressure attempts to move  
in...when winds should lighten up...but think we will hang onto some  
low-level moisture from last night's rain and any of today's drizzle. 
 
Heavy rain concerns Thursday into Friday...hard to ignore the  
signals for a plume of highly anomalous pwats potentially around  
2in, which would be +4 standard deviations above the mean.  Given  
the dynamic system in play...both from the standpoint of a sharp  
trough for better PVA, as well as jet dynamics, with right entrance  
region of an anomalous upper jet (potentially 120-140kts)...do think  
heavy rain will be a concern somewhere across the Midwest/OH Valley  
region. For now...guidance signals the focus for the better pwats  
and subsequent moisture transport are to our south in the OH Valley,  
perhaps scraping southern MI, where there is a better chance for  
either a pivot point and/or multiple rounds of rain. However, I  
can't help but wonder about something developing further north, on  
the cold side of the boundary, particularly as we deal with that  
northern stream bit of energy swinging in and potential for an  
inverted trough/occluded front over the area. If the pattern  
evolution ends up with the northern stream trough scooping up the  
upper low just upstream of us, resulting in a bit more of an  
negative tilt to the incoming system...would have a greater concern  
for more robust precip (storms?) across the region, including  
perhaps in our region. Certainly worth keeping an eye on as we go  
forward.  
 
Strong winds late week/weekend...developing surface cyclone looks to  
become tightly wound as it crosses the region, with pressure  
gradient strengthening in our region between this and anomalous  
surface high over the western US later this week. This signals a  
strengthening of W/NW winds (perhaps to eventually N), strongest  
Thursday night into Friday; winds should taper off a bit going into  
Saturday, but should remain on the breezy side (though not as much  
as Thursday night into Friday). Some deterministic progs quite  
easily suggest potential for sustained winds in the 10-20kt range  
during the day Friday, esp over the waters; main focus for strongest  
winds would be the northern shore of the Yoop and north/northwest- 
facing coastlines, particularly along Lake Michigan. Secondary  
trough axis looking to drop in at some point during the day with the  
troughing aloft...which should enhanced cold advection and gust  
potential...and dynamic setup could suggest some isallobaric input  
to winds/gusts, though not necessarily clear at this time (at least  
for us). Low-level jet looks to run between 30-40kts or so, and  
think this is a reasonable idea for gusts attm, as we should pretty  
easily mix this down. Sounding-derived probabilistic guidance  
suggests a very strong probability of gales on Friday out on the  
lakes, and stronger winds would not be impossible.  
 
Southwest winds may strengthen again going into the latter half of  
the weekend as a system crosses central Canada, though perhaps not  
quite as strong as Thursday night into Friday... and there is also a  
touch less agreement in guidance on how far south the pressure  
gradient will tighten compared to Thursday night-Friday.  
 
First snowflakes of the season Friday night????...with colder air  
mass moving in Friday night into Saturday (1000-500mb layer  
thickness falling below 540dam), would not be entirely surprised if  
a few snowflakes mixed in among the lingering showers/lake effect  
rain showers that will likely be in the area as 925 and 850mb temps  
fall below 0C as early as Friday afternoon. The most likely shot at  
this idea would be in the EUP, but not impossible it could get as  
far south as the interior higher terrain of northern Lower.  
Fortunately the cold air mass does not appear to last long, and we  
should already be warm advecting by Saturday/Saturday afternoon.
