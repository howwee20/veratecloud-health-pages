June 2000

2000 SUMMARY OF ANNUAL PRECIPITATION FOR THE SEMCOG RECORDING RAIN GAUGE NETWORK

Annual precipitation totals over the SEMCOG network during 2000 ranged from a high of 47.40 inches at M-03 in southeastern Macomb County to a low of 30.99 inches at

W-35, in southeastern Wayne County (see ANNUAL 2000 map plus the 2000 Semcog Summary Precipitation and Ranked Monthly and Annual Precipitation Amounts tables). Southwest and east-central Livingston, most of central Oakland, northwestern Wayne, west- and east-central Washtenaw, and southeastern Macomb received more than 40 inches. The secondary network maximum amount, 46.31", was recorded at L-09 in east-central Livingston, while fourteen additional stations recorded more than 40 inches. The regions of lowest amounts (i.e. less than 34 inches) include: central Macomb; northeastern and southeastern Wayne; and one small area of southeastern Oakland.

Compared to the long-term median amounts for each of the stations in the network with more than 15 years of record through 1997 (see map titled 2000 ANNUAL DEPARTURE FROM MEDIAN), 2000 departures ranged from values above the median of 13.10 inches at M-03 in southeastern Macomb and 12.05 inches at O-13 in south-central Oakland to a value below the median of 0.21 inches at W-42 in northeastern Wayne. No other station was below the median. All of Washtenaw County, most of Oakland, about two-thirds of Macomb, and extreme western and east-central Wayne Counties were more than 6 inches above the median, with several of these recording more than 10 inches above the median. In total area, values in 25-35% of the network exceeded median precipitation amounts by more than 8 inches.

The map of ANNUAL 2000 CALCULATED PROBABILITIES shows the percentile ranking of each station’s annual total compared to historical data through 1997, based on a gamma probability distribution function. These map values represent the decimal fraction of years that the annual amount at that station would have been less than or equal to the 2000 amount. For example, the 0.8 isoline encloses all stations in the area where the annual amount would be less than or equal to that received in 2000, during 8 years in 10. The complement of that value, .2, means that in only 2 years in 10 should a station be expected to record more rain than was recorded in 2000. Care must be used in interpreting the values, however, because of the relatively short periods-of-record (as short as 16 years) for some stations. The areas of extreme above-median values resulted in probability values over 0.9 for northwest and east-central Wayne, most of Washtenaw, Livingston, and Oakland, as well as the northern half and a small portion of southeastern of Macomb. The highest probability values were .99+ in southwestern Livingston, east-central Oakland, and east- and west-central Washtenaw. The only area showing a below-median value, W-42 in northeastern Wayne County, had a probability value of 0.49. Only two other stations, W-18 & W-35 (0.53) were lower than the 0.6 probability level. Most of the network was at the 0.8 level or higher, with significant portions of all but Wayne County above the 0.95 level.

Compared to 1999, the network was much wetter in 2000 (see map 2000 ANNUAL CHANGE FROM 1999 ANNUAL). Only W-27 (-0.21") in north-central Wayne County was drier than 1999; O-20 (0.61") in southeastern Oakland and W-35 (1.35") in southeastern Wayne County were the only other stations with less than 2 inches more than the 1999 totals, while most of the network was more than 6 inches wetter than 1999. The greatest changes were: L-05 (+14.34") in west-central Livingston; A-03 (+11.85") in west-central Washtenaw; M-03 (+11.42") and M-16 (+11.01") in southeastern Macomb; O-24 (+11.13") in south-central Oakland; and W-19 (+11.11") in east-central Wayne. An additional three stations, (one each in Washtenaw, Oakland, and Macomb Counties), recorded more than 10 inches above the 1999 levels.

In summary, the year 2000 was a very wet year for the residents of the five county area.
