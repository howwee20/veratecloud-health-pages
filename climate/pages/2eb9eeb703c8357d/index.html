1998 SUMMARY OF ANNUAL PRECIPITATION FOR THE SEMCOG RECORDING RAIN GAUGE NETWORK

Annual precipitation totals over the SEMCOG network during 1998 ranged from a high of 40.11 inches at O-18 in southeast Oakland to a low of 23.23 inches at M-13, in central Macomb (see ANNUAL 1998 map and Ranked Monthly and Annual Precipitation Amounts table). The southern one-half of Washtenaw, southeastern corner of Oakland, and southern one-quarter of Macomb and two small areas in Livingston received more than 32 inches. The secondary network maximum amount, 38.25", was recorded at W-45 in east-central Wayne. Two additional stations recorded more than 37 inches: W-02 (37.51") and O-24 (37.35"). Only one other station was above 36 inches: W-25 (36.36") in north-central Wayne. The regions of lowest amounts (i.e. less than 30 inches) include most of Livingston, Oakland, and Macomb Counties, plus two small areas of Wayne, namely the northeastern and east-central areas.

Compared to the long-term median amounts for each of the stations in the network with more than 15 years of record through 1997 (see map titled 1998 ANNUAL DEPARTURE FROM MEDIAN), 1998 departures ranged from values above the median of 8.88 inches at O-18, in southeastern Oakland, to values below the median of 7.43 inches at O-11 in west-central Oakland and M-13 in central Macomb. Only eight stations in Wayne, six in Oakland, two in Macomb, and four in Washtenaw recorded above-median values. No stations in Livingston recorded above-median amounts. Several locations had departures of more than 3.5 inches below median. Only three areas recorded more than 4.0 inches above the median: the southeastern portion of Oakland and adjacent north-central Wayne; east-central Washtenaw; and east-central Wayne counties. In Macomb County, all but three stations were more than 2 inches below the median values. In total area, about 60-70% of the network was below the median precipitation amounts.

The map of ANNUAL 1998 CALCULATED PROBABILITIES shows the percentile ranking of each station’s annual total compared to historical data through 1997, based on a gamma probability distribution function. These map values represent the decimal fraction of years that the annual amount at that station would have been less than or equal to the 1998 amount. For example, the 0.8 isoline encloses all stations in the area where, during 8 years in 10, the annual amount would be less than or equal to that received in 1998. The complement of that value, .2, is that only 2 years in 10 would be expected to record more rain than was recorded in that area in 1998. Care must be used in interpreting the values, however, because of the relatively short periods of record (16 years) for some stations. The areas of extreme above-median values resulted in probability values of over 0.8 for southeastern Oakland, east-central Wayne, and east-central Washtenaw. The highest probability values were .93 at O-18 in southeast Oakland and .87 (W-45) in east-central Wayne. Stations A-04 and A-05, with 0.84 and 0.81, respectively, were the only other stations above 0.8. The areas of extreme below-median values were in Livingston and Macomb, where the calculated probabilities were less than .2 for several stations.

Compared to 1997, the network was generally drier in 1998, (see map 1998 ANNUAL CHANGE FROM 1997 ANNUAL) with only the northwest corner of Livingston, east-central Washtenaw and the adjacent areas of west-central Wayne being wetter than in 1997. The greatest changes were in northeast Wayne, with W-31 (-8.76"), W-42 (-8.58") & W-33 (-8.46"). Only one additional station, O-13 (-8.17") in southwest Oakland, was below -8 inches. Wayne had the highest positive deviation, also: W-48 (1.98")
