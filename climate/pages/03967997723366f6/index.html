1999 SUMMARY OF ANNUAL PRECIPITATION FOR THE SEMCOG RECORDING RAIN GAUGE NETWORK

Annual precipitation totals over the SEMCOG network during 1999 ranged from a high of 36.96 inches at L-09 in east-central Livingston to a low of 25.75 inches at M-02, in east-central Macomb (see ANNUAL 1999 map plus the 1999 Semcog Summary Precipitation and Ranked Monthly and Annual Precipitation Amounts tables). The west-central and east-central Livingston, most of central Oakland, north-central Wayne, and three small areas in the southern and eastern areas of Macomb received more than 32 inches. The secondary network maximum amount, 35.98", was recorded at M-03 in southern Macomb. Four additional stations recorded more than 35 inches: W-27 (35.77"); O-13 (35.31"); L-10 (35.27"); and O-18 (35.09"). The regions of lowest amounts (i.e. less than 30 inches) include: northwest, central, and southern Livingston; central, southwestern and southeastern Macomb; Washtenaw except for the northeastern corner; the southern half and northeastern Wayne; and one small area of southeastern Oakland.

Compared to the long-term median amounts for each of the stations in the network with more than 15 years of record through 1997 (see map titled 1999 ANNUAL DEPARTURE FROM MEDIAN), 1999 departures ranged from values above the median of 4.39 inches at W-27 in north-central Wayne, and 4.20 inches at O-13 in south-central Oakland, to values below the median of 7.00 inches at W-42 in northeastern Wayne. Only three other stations were more than 5 inches below the median [W-08 (-5.40"); W-18 (-5.30"); and W-43 (-5.19")], while W-46 at -4.98" was close behind. Only one station in each of Livingston and Washtenaw Counties recorded above-median amounts, while much of Oakland, central and north-central Wayne, and west-central and northwestern Macomb were all above the median. The driest areas were in southeastern Macomb and eastern Wayne Counties. In total area, about 50-60% of the network was below the median precipitation amounts.

The map of ANNUAL 1999 CALCULATED PROBABILITIES shows the percentile ranking of each station’s annual total compared to historical data through 1997, based on a gamma probability distribution function. These map values represent the decimal fraction of years that the annual amount at that station would have been less than or equal to the 1999 amount. For example, the 0.8 isoline encloses all stations in the area where, during 8 years in 10, the annual amount would be less than or equal to that received in 1999. The complement of that value, .2, means that only 2 years in 10 would be expected to record more rain than was recorded in that area in 1999. Care must be used in interpreting the values, however, because of the relatively short periods-of-record (as short as 16 years) for some stations. The areas of extreme above-median values resulted in probability values over 0.8 for north-central Wayne and over 0.7 for a surrounding area extending into south-central Oakland. The highest probability values were .80 at W-27 and .78 at O-13 in southwestern Oakland. The areas of extreme below-median values included: three eastern sections of Wayne; southern and southwestern Livingston; and a small southeastern section of Macomb, where the calculated probabilities were less than .2 for several stations.

Compared to 1998, the network was generally wetter in 1999 (see map 1999 ANNUAL CHANGE FROM 1998 ANNUAL), with all of Livingston except the northwest and southwest corners, most of Oakland except the southeastern corner, most of Macomb except the southwestern and southeastern corners, and central Wayne being wetter than in 1998. The greatest changes were: in northeast Oakland, O-09 (5.54"); in east-central Macomb, M-14 (5.52"); and in west-central Oakland O-11 (5.38"). One other station in Oakland and three others in Macomb showed departures of more than 5 inches. An additional two stations in Livingston and one in Oakland recorded more than four inches above 1998 amounts. Three stations were more than 5 inches below the amount recorded in 1998: A-04 (-5.21") in east-central Washtenaw; W-45 (-5.10") in east-central Wayne; and O-18 (-5.02") in southeast Oakland. Only two other stations, A-05 (-4.08") in east-central Washtenaw and nearby W-48 (-4.05") in west-central Wayne, were more than four inches below 1998 values.
